Why NEMO exists

The problem it removes from the operator's watch.

Manual sonar review is slow. NEMO highlights candidates automatically, reducing the time operators spend staring at vast stretches of empty seabed.

Natural features mimic objects. The verification stage filters false positives like rocks and sand ripples, so only high-confidence targets reach review.

A detection needs context

Every contact ships as evidence, not a bare count.

Reports include coordinates, measurements, and image evidence, integrating smoothly with downstream inspection teams.

Open the analysis station to watch a frame move through the pipeline, or the reports station to filter the full contact log.

Architecture

Seven stages from ping to evidence.

01 · Input[Side-Scan Sonar Input]
↓
02 · Condition[Preprocessing · Grayscale · 1-Channel · 640×640]
↓
03 · Propose[YOLO11n-1C Candidate Detector]
↓
04 · Extract[Candidate Crop Extraction]
↓
05 · Verify[MobileNetV3-Small Verifier]
↓
06 · Locate[Metadata Association and Geotagging]
↓
07 · Report[Anomaly Report: CSV/JSON + Dashboard]
Two-model design

YOLO11n finds candidate objects. Its job is localization and candidate generation.

MobileNetV3 examines cropped candidate regions. Classifies as target-like or background.

It does not independently establish geographic location.

Limitations and next steps
  • Expand hard-negative examples: rocks, ridges, shadows, seabed structures
  • Validate on unseen survey missions and representative sonar data
  • Improve coordinate association using reliable ping/navigation metadata
Standing order NEMO’s goal: turn sonar detections into reviewable, location-aware evidence for underwater inspection and cleanup decisions.
