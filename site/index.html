The problem

Debris sinks. Attention shouldn't have to.

Lost nets, wreckage, and dumped waste settle onto the seabed where divers can't patrol and cameras can't reach. Side-scan sonar can see it. But every hour of survey produces hours of imagery, and almost all of it is empty seabed.

Today, finding debris means operators staring at vast stretches of nothing, ping by ping, hoping fatigue doesn't bury a contact. It is slow, expensive, and misses things, while ghost gear keeps fishing and wreckage keeps leaching.

Worse, the seabed itself mimics debris. Rocks, ridges, sand ripples, and shadows all look target-like to a tired eye, and to a naive detector. Every false alarm that reaches a dive team burns fuel, daylight, and trust.

NEMO exists to break that tradeoff: machine eyes for the empty miles, human judgment for the contacts that matter.

How NEMO tackles it

Propose boldly. Verify skeptically.

A two-model pipeline tuned for side-scan sonar. The first model is rewarded for never missing; the second is rewarded for never crying wolf.

01 / PROPOSE
YOLO11n · 1-CHANNEL · 640 × 640

The scout that never blinks

A YOLO11n detector retuned for single-channel sonar sweeps every frame and localizes candidate objects: anything target-like, with a confidence score. Its job is recall: miss nothing, decide nothing.

02 / VERIFY
MOBILENETV3-SMALL · 128 × 128 CROPS

The skeptic that kills false alarms

Each candidate is cropped and handed to a MobileNetV3 verifier that classifies it artificial or natural. Rocks, ridges, and ripples die here. Only high-confidence targets reach your screen.

03 / EQUIP
CSV + JSON + CONSOLE

Evidence a crew can act on

Survivors are fused with detector confidence, acoustic features and, when the mission carries navigation data, real coordinates. Missions persist with reports you can download, filter, and dispatch from. No coordinates invented, ever.
